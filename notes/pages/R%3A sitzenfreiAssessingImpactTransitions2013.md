- [[Abstract]]
	- Traditional urban water management relies on centrally organised infrastructure, primarily drainage and water distribution networks. To meet upcoming challenges such as climate change, rapid urban growth and shrinkage, and water scarcity, water infrastructure needs to become more flexible, adaptable and sustainable (e.g., SUDS, WSUD, LID, BMP). This paper uses VIBe (Virtual Infrastructure Benchmarking), a tool for stochastic generation of urban water systems at city scale, to perform an integrated city-scale analysis of the transition from centralised to decentralised urban water management. The approach allows conclusions to be drawn regarding the technical effects of this transition on existing infrastructure and demonstrates how virtual datasets can assist with the model-building process.
